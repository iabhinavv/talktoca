Old vs new tax regime for salaried taxpayers

Which regime to choose for FY 2025-26 returns and FY 2026-27 planning, with a worked example on a ₹15 lakh salary.

The new tax regime is the default. Salaried taxpayers can still choose the old regime each year, but it pays only when deductions are large. Below are both regimes, a worked example, and what the Income-tax Act, 2025 changes.

Which law applies to which year

  • FY 2025-26 (returns filed in 2026): Income-tax Act, 1961.
  • FY 2026-27 onwards: Income-tax Act, 2025, in force from 1 April 2026. It replaces "previous year" and "assessment year" with a single "tax year" and renumbers sections — the rebate under section 87A becomes section 156, section 80C becomes section 123, and Form 16 becomes Form 130.

Budget 2026 kept the new-regime slabs unchanged for FY 2026-27, so the rates below apply to both years.

  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 on salary income.
  • Rebate: a resident individual with taxable income up to ₹12 lakh pays no tax (rebate up to ₹60,000). With the standard deduction, salary up to ₹12.75 lakh is effectively tax-free. The rebate does not cover tax on special-rate income such as certain capital gains.
  • Deductions: few — mainly the employer's NPS contribution. No 80C, 80D, HRA or home-loan interest on a self-occupied house.

The basic exemption is ₹3 lakh for senior citizens (60–79) and ₹5 lakh from 80. The standard deduction is ₹50,000, and deductions include 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh), 80D health insurance, HRA, home-loan interest (up to ₹2 lakh for a self-occupied house) and 80CCD(1B) NPS (up to ₹50,000).

Worked example: salary of ₹15 lakh

Assume old-regime deductions of ₹1.5 lakh under 80C and ₹25,000 under 80D, and no HRA or home loan.

Here the new regime saves about ₹1.05 lakh. The old regime becomes competitive only when HRA and home-loan interest add several lakh more in deductions, so run both calculations with your actual figures before choosing.

How to choose and switch

  • Salaried, no business income: you can pick either regime every year when filing. Tell your employer your choice at the start of the year so TDS is right; you can still change it in the return.
  • With business or professional income: switching is restricted — once you leave the new regime you can return to it only once.

If you missed the July 2026 deadline

The due date for non-audit individual returns for FY 2025-26 was 31 July 2026. A belated return can still be filed until 31 December 2026, with a late fee of ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh) plus interest on unpaid tax. A belated return cannot carry forward most losses.

Frequently asked questions

Does the ₹12 lakh rebate apply to NRIs?

No. The rebate is available only to resident individuals.

My employer deducted TDS under the old regime. Can I file under the new one?

Yes, if you have no business income. Any excess TDS is refunded after the return is processed.
