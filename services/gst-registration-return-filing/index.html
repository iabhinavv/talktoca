GST Registration & Return Filing

Registration, monthly and quarterly returns, input credit reconciliation and notices.

GST is where most Indian businesses meet the tax system every month, and where most avoidable money is lost — to late fees, to input tax credit that was claimed without matching, and to notices that a clean reconciliation would have prevented. We handle registration, the monthly and quarterly cycle, annual returns and departmental correspondence.

Who has to register for GST

Registration becomes compulsory once aggregate turnover crosses the threshold — broadly ₹40 lakh for suppliers of goods and ₹20 lakh for suppliers of services in normal category states, with lower limits for special category states. Aggregate turnover is computed across all your registrations on the same PAN, all India, which surprises businesses running more than one vertical.

Threshold aside, registration is required regardless of turnover in several situations:

  • Making inter-state taxable supplies of goods
  • Selling through an e-commerce operator that collects tax at source
  • Being liable to pay tax under reverse charge
  • Acting as an agent, input service distributor, or casual or non-resident taxable person

A tricity trap worth knowing. Chandigarh's GST state code is 04, Punjab's is 03 and Haryana's is 06. A supply from your Chandigarh office to your Zirakpur or Panchkula premises is an inter-state supply attracting IGST, and may require a separate registration — despite being a twenty-minute drive. Structure this before you take the second premises, not after.

Documents for GST registration

  • PAN of the business and of the proprietor, partners or directors
  • Aadhaar of the authorised signatory, for e-KYC authentication
  • Certificate of incorporation or partnership deed, as applicable
  • Proof of the principal place of business — utility bill, rent agreement and owner's NOC
  • Bank account details: a cancelled cheque or the first page of a statement
  • Photographs and board resolution or authorisation letter for the signatory

Aadhaar authentication speeds things up substantially. Where it is not completed, the application usually goes to physical verification of premises, which adds time.

Input tax credit: where the real money is

Credit is the part of GST that rewards discipline and punishes assumption. A few rules govern most of the disputes we see:

  • Credit is available only where the invoice appears in your auto-populated GSTR-2B. If your supplier has not filed, you do not have the credit — however valid your invoice looks.
  • Payment to the supplier must be made within the prescribed period, or the credit is reversed with interest.
  • Certain credits are blocked outright — motor vehicles in most cases, works contract for immovable property, personal consumption and goods lost or written off.
  • There is a hard cut-off for claiming credit for a financial year, after which it lapses permanently. Reconciling in March, when the year is nearly closed, is far too late.

We reconcile purchase registers against GSTR-2B every month, flag suppliers who are not filing so you can chase them while the invoice is still current, and keep the running difference visible instead of discovering it at year end.

E-invoicing and e-way bills

Businesses above the prescribed aggregate turnover must generate e-invoices with an Invoice Reference Number for B2B supplies; an invoice without a valid IRN is not a valid tax invoice, and your customer's credit depends on it. Separately, movement of goods above the prescribed consignment value requires an e-way bill. Both are areas where the compliance is mechanical but the consequence of getting it wrong lands on the customer's credit and, eventually, on your relationship with them.

Notices, mismatches and departmental representation

The commonest GST notices follow a predictable pattern: a difference between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, a difference between credit claimed in GSTR-3B and credit available in GSTR-2B, or a mismatch between GST turnover and the turnover declared in your income tax return. Most are answerable with a clean reconciliation and a reply filed within the deadline. We prepare the working, draft the reply, file it, and appear before the authority where a hearing is called.

Frequently asked questions

What is the GST registration limit in India?

Broadly, ₹40 lakh of aggregate turnover for suppliers of goods and ₹20 lakh for suppliers of services in normal category states, with lower limits in special category states. Aggregate turnover is calculated on the PAN across all registrations in India, so multiple businesses under the same PAN are added together. Registration is compulsory regardless of turnover for inter-state supply of goods, supplies through e-commerce operators, and certain other categories.

How long does GST registration take?

Where Aadhaar authentication is completed successfully, registration is typically granted within a few working days. Where it is not, the application usually goes for physical verification of the business premises, which extends the timeline. Incomplete address proof is the most common reason an application is queried.

What happens if I file GST returns late?

Late fees accrue for each day of delay for each return, and interest runs on any tax paid late. Beyond the money, a pattern of late filing blocks your customers from claiming credit on your invoices, which is usually the more expensive consequence. Prolonged non-filing can lead to cancellation of the registration.

Do I have to file GST returns if I had no sales?

Yes. A nil return is still a return. An active GSTIN with no transactions in the period must file, and the late fee applies for nil returns too. If a registration is genuinely no longer needed, the correct course is to apply for cancellation rather than to stop filing.

Can I claim input tax credit if my supplier has not filed their return?

In practice, no. Credit is restricted to invoices appearing in your auto-populated GSTR-2B. If the supplier has not filed their GSTR-1, the invoice does not appear and the credit is not available to you, however genuine the purchase. This is why we reconcile monthly — so you can chase the supplier while it can still be fixed.

Should I opt for the composition scheme?

It depends on who your customers are. The composition scheme reduces your compliance and your rate, but you cannot charge GST on your invoices and your customers cannot claim credit from you. That is fine if you sell to end consumers and fatal if you sell to businesses. You also cannot claim input credit on your own purchases. We run the comparison on your actual numbers before you elect.
