Virtual CFO Services

Monthly MIS, cash-flow forecasting, budgeting and investor reporting for growing businesses.

There is a stage most growing businesses hit where the accountant is keeping up but nobody is actually running the numbers. Revenue is growing, cash is tight, and no one can say confidently which customer or product is funding the rest. That is the gap a virtual CFO fills — senior financial judgement, applied monthly, without a full-time hire at a full-time salary.

What a virtual CFO actually does

  • Monthly management accounts closed on a fixed date, with variance against budget and the prior period explained rather than just presented.
  • Cash-flow forecasting — a rolling view of what is coming in and going out, so a shortfall is visible weeks ahead instead of on the day.
  • Budgeting and planning — an annual budget the business is actually run against, with quarterly re-forecasts as reality diverges.
  • Unit economics — contribution by product, service line, customer or branch, after allocating overheads honestly.
  • Working capital management — debtor days, creditor days and inventory holding, and the specific interventions that move them.
  • Pricing and margin analysis — what a discount actually costs once volume effects are counted.
  • Investor and lender reporting — board packs and bank submissions that anticipate the questions rather than inviting them.
  • Controls and process — approval limits, segregation of duties, and the discipline that keeps a growing business from losing money quietly.

Who this is for

Businesses roughly in the range where a full-time CFO is not yet justified but the founder is already the bottleneck on financial decisions. In practice that means funded startups after their first round, family businesses professionalising, manufacturers with complex working capital, and services firms whose profitability varies sharply by client without anyone knowing exactly why.

A useful test. If you cannot answer, this month, which of your customers or products is unprofitable and by how much — you have a book-keeper, not a finance function. That is the gap this service closes.

How the engagement runs

  1. Diagnostic — two to three weeks reviewing the books, the reporting and the controls as they are, and setting out plainly what is missing.
  2. Foundations — a chart of accounts that supports decisions, a monthly close calendar, and an MIS pack designed around the handful of numbers that actually drive your business.
  3. Monthly cycle — close, MIS, a review meeting where the numbers are discussed and decisions are taken, and a rolling cash-flow update.
  4. Quarterly — re-forecast, deeper margin and working capital review, and board or investor reporting.
  5. As needed — funding support, valuation, due diligence readiness and lender negotiations.

Funding, valuation and due diligence support

When you raise capital or take on debt, the finance function is what is being examined. We prepare the financial model and projections, assemble the data room, run pre-diligence to find the problems before the investor does, prepare valuation reports where they are required for pricing or regulatory filings, and sit in on the diligence process. Businesses whose books are already clean close faster and negotiate from a stronger position — the difference is rarely the numbers themselves, it is how much of them needs explaining.

How this differs from your accountant

Your accountant records what happened and files what is due, and that work remains essential. A virtual CFO uses the same data to answer forward-looking questions: whether to take the order, hire the team, extend the credit, or raise the round. Frequently asked questions

What is a virtual CFO?

A virtual CFO is an experienced finance professional engaged on a part-time or retained basis to do the work a full-time chief financial officer would do — monthly management reporting, cash-flow forecasting, budgeting, margin analysis, controls, and investor or lender reporting — without the cost of a full-time senior hire.

How is a virtual CFO different from an accountant or book-keeper?

A book-keeper records transactions and an accountant prepares and files statutory returns. Both are backward-looking by nature and both are necessary. A virtual CFO uses that data to answer forward-looking questions: whether the business can fund a hire, which product line is subsidising the others, when cash will get tight, and what a lender or investor will make of the numbers.

What size of business needs a virtual CFO?

Typically one where the founder is spending significant time on financial decisions without reliable numbers to base them on, but where a full-time CFO salary is not yet justified. Funded startups after a first round, family businesses professionalising, and manufacturers with complex working capital cycles are the most common profiles.

Can you work alongside our existing accountant?

Yes, and it is a common arrangement. We take the data your accountant produces, add the reporting and analysis layer on top, and give your team clearer instructions on what we need and when. If you would rather consolidate compliance and CFO work with one firm, we can do that too.

Will you help us raise funding?

We prepare the financial model and projections, build the data room, run a pre-diligence review to surface issues before an investor does, prepare valuation reports where required, and support you through the diligence process. We do not act as placement agents or introduce investors — our role is to make sure the numbers hold up when they look.
