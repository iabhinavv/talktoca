Audit & Assurance

Statutory audit, tax audit, internal audit and stock audit conducted to withstand scrutiny.

An audit is worth more than the signature at the end of it. Done properly, it is the one time each year that someone independent examines how money, stock and credit actually move through your business — and tells you where they are leaking. We conduct statutory, tax, internal and stock audits for companies, LLPs and firms across Chandigarh and beyond.

Statutory audit under the Companies Act

Every company incorporated in India must have its financial statements audited each year, regardless of turnover or whether it traded at all. The auditor is appointed by the members, the appointment is filed with the Registrar in Form ADT-1, and the audited statements go on to form part of the AOC-4 filing.

Our statutory audit covers the recognition and measurement of revenue and expenses, verification of assets and liabilities, related party transactions and their disclosure, compliance with the applicable accounting standards, and the specific reporting the auditor's report requires.

Tax audit under section 44AB

A tax audit applies where business turnover exceeds the prescribed limit — with a substantially higher limit where both cash receipts and cash payments remain within the prescribed small proportion of the total — or where professional gross receipts exceed the prescribed limit. It also applies in certain cases where income is declared below the presumptive rate.

The report is filed in the prescribed forms with the detailed annexure, ordinarily before the return is filed, and failure to obtain it attracts a penalty linked to turnover. The annexure asks searching questions about cash transactions, loans, disallowable payments and TDS compliance, which is why a tax audit tends to surface issues that book-keeping alone did not.

Internal audit

Required for certain classes of companies and voluntarily adopted by many others once they reach a size where the promoter cannot personally see every transaction. Internal audit is about controls rather than opinion: whether purchase orders precede purchases, whether receipts are banked promptly, whether credit limits are respected, whether stock records match the floor, and whether the people approving payments are different from the people making them.

Stock, fixed asset and process audits

For manufacturers, distributors and retailers, inventory is usually the largest number on the balance sheet and the least verified. We conduct physical stock verification, reconcile it to the records, examine valuation and slow-moving or obsolete provisioning, and verify fixed asset existence against the register. Lenders frequently require this independently.

GST audit and departmental audit support

Where turnover crosses the prescribed limit, the annual reconciliation statement in GSTR-9C must be filed alongside the annual return. Separately, the department conducts its own audits and inspections. We prepare the reconciliations, assemble the records the officer will ask for, and represent you through the process.

How we run an audit

  1. Planning — understanding the business, the transaction cycles and where material misstatement is actually likely, rather than applying a generic checklist.
  2. Control evaluation — testing whether the controls you believe exist are operating.
  3. Substantive testing — verification of balances, third-party confirmations, physical verification and analytical review against prior periods and industry norms.
  4. Reporting — the statutory report, plus a management letter setting out control weaknesses and what to do about them. That letter is where most of the practical value sits.
  5. Follow-up — checking in the following cycle whether the points raised were acted on.

A note on independence. Professional standards restrict an auditor from also performing certain services for the same audit client. Where that applies, we will tell you plainly and help you arrange the other work elsewhere. It is not a technicality — it is what makes the opinion worth anything.

Frequently asked questions

Does every company need a statutory audit?

Yes. Every company registered in India must have its financial statements audited each year, regardless of turnover, and even if it had no transactions during the year. This is a significant difference from a proprietorship or partnership, where audit is triggered only by turnover thresholds, and it is a recurring cost founders should factor in before choosing the company structure.

When is a tax audit required?

A tax audit under section 44AB is triggered where business turnover exceeds the prescribed limit — with a substantially higher limit where both cash receipts and cash payments stay within the prescribed small proportion of the total — or where professional gross receipts exceed the prescribed limit. It can also apply where income is declared below the presumptive rate. We assess applicability on your actual figures well before the deadline.

Is an LLP required to be audited?

An LLP must have its accounts audited once turnover or partner contribution crosses the prescribed limits. Below those limits, audit is not mandatory under the LLP Act, although a tax audit may still apply separately under the Income Tax Act, and lenders or investors frequently ask for audited accounts regardless.

What is the penalty for not getting a tax audit done?

A penalty linked to turnover, subject to a prescribed ceiling, unless there is reasonable cause. The practical consequence is usually broader: the return cannot be filed correctly without the report, which cascades into late filing and the loss of certain carry-forward benefits.

Can the same firm do our audit and our accounting?

Not for a statutory audit client — professional standards restrict an auditor from providing certain other services to the same entity, precisely so the opinion stays independent. Where this applies to you we will say so up front and help you arrange the other work with a different firm.

What documents do you need to start an audit?

Trial balance and ledgers for the year, bank statements and reconciliations, purchase and sales registers, GST returns filed, TDS returns and challans, fixed asset register, inventory records, loan agreements and sanction letters, statutory registers and minutes, and the prior year's audited financials. We send a tailored request list once we understand the business.
