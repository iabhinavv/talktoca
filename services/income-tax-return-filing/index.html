Income Tax Return Filing

ITR filing and year-round tax planning for companies, firms, professionals and individuals.

Filing the return is the visible part. The work that determines what the return says happens through the year — deciding the regime, paying advance tax on time, deducting TDS correctly, and keeping the documentation that supports each claim. We file for companies, LLPs, firms, professionals, salaried individuals and NRIs, and we do the planning that makes the filing straightforward.

Who we file for

  • Companies and LLPs — with the tax audit where applicable, and reconciliation between books, GST turnover and the return.
  • Partnership firms and proprietorships — including presumptive taxation where it genuinely suits the business.
  • Professionals and consultants — doctors, architects, lawyers, freelancers and agencies, weighing presumptive schemes against maintaining books.
  • Salaried individuals — with capital gains, house property, foreign assets or multiple Form 16s.
  • NRIs — residential status, income taxable in India, DTAA relief, TDS on property sales under section 195 and repatriation certificates.

Old regime or new regime?

The new regime under section 115BAC is now the default; the old regime must be opted into. The comparison is not a matter of principle — it is arithmetic, and it changes as your deductions change. For someone with a home loan, substantial 80C investments, HRA and a health insurance premium, the old regime can still come out ahead. For someone with few deductions, the new regime usually wins. We run both on your actual numbers before filing rather than assuming.

Note that businesses and professionals face restrictions on switching back and forth between regimes in later years, so the first choice carries more weight for them than for salaried taxpayers.

Presumptive taxation: useful, but not for everyone

Sections 44AD and 44ADA let eligible small businesses and professionals declare income at a prescribed percentage of turnover or gross receipts, with relief from maintaining detailed books. The turnover limits are higher where cash receipts stay within the prescribed small proportion of total receipts — which is a deliberate nudge towards banking channels.

It is genuinely simpler. But if your actual margin is lower than the deemed rate, you pay tax on profit you never earned; and once you opt out, you are locked out for a period. It suits high-margin professional practices far better than thin-margin trading businesses. We compare the presumptive outcome against actual results before recommending it.

Tax audit under section 44AB

A tax audit is triggered when business turnover exceeds the prescribed limit — with a substantially higher limit available where both cash receipts and cash payments stay within the prescribed small proportion of the total — or where professional gross receipts exceed the prescribed limit. It also applies in certain cases where a taxpayer declares income below the presumptive rate. The report is filed before the return, and missing it carries a penalty linked to turnover. See audit and assurance for how we run it.

Notices, assessments and appeals

Most communications from the department are routine and answerable: intimation of an arithmetic difference, a mismatch between the return and Form 26AS or the Annual Information Statement, a query under limited scrutiny, or a TDS default flagged on TRACES. Proceedings are largely faceless now, which means the quality of the written submission carries the case. We prepare the reconciliation, draft the response, file it within the window, and take the matter to appeal where the position is worth defending.

Check your AIS before you file, not after. The Annual Information Statement aggregates what third parties have reported about you — interest, dividends, securities transactions, property dealings, large cash deposits. A return that does not reconcile with it is the single most common trigger for a query. We reconcile it as a matter of course.

Frequently asked questions

What is the last date for filing an income tax return?

For taxpayers not subject to audit, the ordinary due date is 31 July following the end of the financial year. Where a tax audit applies, it is 31 October, and 30 November where transfer pricing provisions apply. A belated or revised return can ordinarily be filed up to 31 December of the assessment year. The department occasionally extends these dates, so we confirm the operative deadline for your case.

What happens if I miss the ITR deadline?

You can still file a belated return, but a late filing fee applies and interest runs on unpaid tax. More importantly, filing late restricts your ability to carry forward certain losses, which can be far more expensive than the fee itself for a business that had a loss year.

Which income tax regime should I choose?

The new regime is the default and generally favours taxpayers with few deductions. The old regime can still be better where you have significant deductions — a home loan, 80C investments, HRA, health insurance. It is a calculation, not a principle, and we run both on your figures before filing. Business and professional taxpayers should note the restrictions on switching between regimes in later years.

Do I need to file a return if my income is below the taxable limit?

Often yes. Filing is required in a number of situations irrespective of income level — for example where you hold foreign assets, where prescribed high-value transactions or deposits have occurred, or where TDS has been deducted and you want the refund. Filing also creates the income record that banks and visa authorities ask for.

How is a Chartered Accountant different from a filing website?

A portal validates arithmetic. A CA decides the positions — which regime, which head of income, whether a receipt is capital or revenue, whether presumptive taxation genuinely suits you, and what documentation you need to hold if the claim is questioned three years later. The filing is the easy part; standing behind the position is the work.

Can you handle a scrutiny notice I have already received?

Yes, and it is common for clients to come to us at that point. Bring the notice, the return it relates to and the supporting records. We will tell you what the department is actually asking, what the realistic outcome is, and what it will cost to respond properly — before you commit.
