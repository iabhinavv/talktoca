ROC & Annual Compliance

AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC, DPT-3, MSME-1 and event-based filings, tracked and filed on time.

Company law compliance is unforgiving in a specific way: the penalties are usually per day, they attach to the company and to the officers personally, and there is no reasonable-cause escape for simply having forgotten. Most of it is entirely mechanical. We run the calendar so it never becomes a problem.

Event-based filings

Beyond the annual cycle, specific corporate actions carry their own forms and their own clocks — usually 15 to 30 days from the event. Missing them is how companies end up paying additional fees that dwarf the original filing cost:

  • Appointment, resignation or change of directors (DIR-12)
  • Allotment of shares and the return of allotment (PAS-3)
  • Change of registered office, within or across jurisdictions (INC-22, INC-23)
  • Increase in authorised share capital and alteration of the memorandum (SH-7)
  • Creation, modification or satisfaction of charges over assets (CHG-1, CHG-4)
  • Declaration of commencement of business (INC-20A) within 180 days of incorporation
  • Changes to the objects clause, name or articles

Registers and minutes — the part everyone skips

The Companies Act requires companies to maintain statutory registers — members, directors, charges, related party contracts — and to record properly signed minutes of board and general meetings. These are rarely inspected in an ordinary year and become critical the moment they matter: at due diligence, in an adjudication, when a bank takes a charge, or in a dispute between shareholders.

Reconstructing three years of minutes under time pressure during a funding round is a characteristic and avoidable panic. We maintain them contemporaneously.

Frequently asked questions

What are the annual compliance requirements for a private limited company?

At minimum: holding the required board meetings and the AGM, getting the accounts audited, filing AOC-4 with the financial statements and MGT-7 or MGT-7A as the annual return, filing ADT-1 for the auditor's appointment, DIR-3 KYC for each director, DPT-3, and MSME-1 where amounts are outstanding to MSME suppliers. The income tax return and any GST and TDS filings run alongside these.

What is the penalty for late ROC filing?

Late filings attract additional fees that increase with the length of the delay, and the underlying default can carry penalties on the company and on the officers in default personally. Because several of these are computed per day, a filing forgotten for a year typically costs many times the original fee.

What happens if a company does not file for several years?

The Registrar can strike the company off the register, and directors of a company that has failed to file for a continuous period can be disqualified from holding directorships. Both are reversible in appropriate cases — through condonation or restoration proceedings — but the process is considerably more expensive than the filings would have been.

Does a dormant company with no transactions still have to file?

Yes. A company with no business activity still has to hold its meetings, have its accounts audited and make its annual filings. If the company is genuinely not going to trade, the cleaner options are applying for dormant status or striking it off voluntarily — either is cheaper than years of nil compliance.

Is ROC compliance the same for an LLP?

No, and it is lighter. An LLP files Form 11 as its annual return and Form 8 as its statement of account and solvency, and has no requirement to hold board meetings or an AGM. Audit is required only once turnover or contribution crosses the prescribed limits. This is one of the main practical reasons to prefer an LLP where outside investment is not planned.
