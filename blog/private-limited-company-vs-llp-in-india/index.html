Private Limited company vs LLP: which should you register?

The structure decision hinges on one question more than any other — whether you will raise outside capital. Here is how the rest of the comparison falls out.

This decision gets made badly more often than any other in Indian business formation, usually on the basis of what a friend did or what a registration website recommended. The two structures are genuinely different, and the right answer depends almost entirely on one question: are you going to raise outside capital?

The short version

Choose a private limited company if…

  • You intend to raise external funding. This is close to decisive. Venture investors and most angels subscribe to equity shares; the instruments they use assume a company. Converting an LLP to a company mid-raise is possible but adds months at exactly the wrong time.
  • You want to give employees equity. ESOPs work in a company structure.
  • Your customers are large corporates or government bodies. Procurement teams are more comfortable with companies, and some tenders effectively require one.
  • There will be multiple founders with differing shareholdings that may change. Share transfers are cleaner than amending an LLP agreement each time.

Choose an LLP if…

  • It is a professional practice or a services firm with no plans for outside capital. This is what the structure was designed for.
  • It is a family business or a closely held venture where the partners are stable and the priority is limited liability without administrative overhead.
  • You want to hold assets or run a joint venture with limited compliance.
  • Annual running cost genuinely matters. No mandatory audit below the prescribed limits and two annual filings instead of a full secretarial calendar is a meaningful saving, year after year.

The cost people forget. A private limited company must be audited every single year even if it did no business at all. Founders who incorporate a company "to look serious" and then trade at ₹5 lakh of revenue are paying a permanent fixed cost for an option they may never exercise. If funding is genuinely not on the roadmap, that money is better spent elsewhere.

What about a One Person Company?

An OPC gives a single founder corporate status and limited liability, with a nominee who steps in on death or incapacity. It carries most of a company's compliance obligations, including audit. It suits a solo founder who wants the corporate form and does not want a partner. If external funding is in view, a private limited company with a co-founder or a nominal second shareholder is usually the better starting point.

Can you convert later?

Yes — an LLP can be converted into a private limited company and vice versa, subject to conditions. But conversion means fresh approvals, transfer of assets and contracts, new registrations under GST and other laws, and potential tax consequences on the transfer itself. It is a project, not a form. Choosing correctly at the start is materially cheaper.

How we would decide it with you

The conversation takes about half an hour and covers four things: whether outside capital is realistically on the horizon in the next three years, who your customers will be, what liability the business is genuinely exposed to, and what ongoing compliance cost you are prepared to carry. The answer usually falls out of those four.

Frequently asked questions

Is an LLP cheaper to run than a private limited company?

Yes, materially. An LLP has no mandatory audit below the prescribed turnover and contribution limits, files two annual returns rather than a full secretarial calendar, and has no statutory meeting requirements. Across several years the difference is significant for a small business.

Can an LLP raise venture capital funding?

In practice, no. Venture investors subscribe to equity shares and use instruments that assume a company structure. If you expect to raise institutional capital, incorporate as a private limited company from the start rather than planning to convert mid-raise.

Can a private limited company be converted into an LLP?

Conversion is possible in either direction subject to conditions, but it involves fresh approvals, transfer of assets and contracts, new registrations and potential tax consequences. Treat it as a last resort rather than a plan.

How many people are needed to start each?

A private limited company needs at least two shareholders and two directors, with at least one director resident in India. An LLP needs at least two partners, of whom at least two are designated partners with at least one resident in India. A single founder who wants corporate form can consider a One Person Company.
